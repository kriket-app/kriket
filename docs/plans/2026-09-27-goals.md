## Why

Sam asks "am I going to be OK?" in three forms (row 2): until the next pay, by the end of the month, and by the date of the thing they are saving for. Iteration 2 answers the first two: the sentence at the top of the overview says whether the balance goes under zero, when, and when it comes back. The third form has no answer yet. Sam wants $1,000 for the trip home by Dec 20, and today has to find Dec 20 on the chart, read the line by eye, and do the subtraction. A goal is a name, an amount, and a date; kriket reads the forecast on that date, says it in one line under the sentence, and marks the spot on the chart. It is the third concept from the pitch (income, expenses, goals) and the last piece of the question Sam opens the app to ask.

## What goals deliver on screen (on top of the iteration 2 overview)

- **The goal line,** under the answer sentence, inside the answer card, above a thin divider: "**Trip home** · Expected $1,240 by Dec 20, $240 over your $1,000 goal." Then a short note from the worst or best case when a range is in play ("The worst case is $120 short."), then "Edit goal ›". It shows at every window (30, 90, 180 days), because it is read from a forecast that reaches the goal date, not from the chart's window. With no goal, the same spot holds one muted link: "Saving for something? Set a goal ›". The balances page's answer card shows no goal line (its forecasts start from older check-ins).
- **The target on the chart:** a ring at the goal amount on the goal date, a thin dashed line from it down to the bottom of the chart, and a small label "Goal $1,000". The chart's scale stretches to include the goal amount, and the legend gains "Goal". The target is drawn only when the goal date is inside the chart; otherwise the goal line adds "See it on the chart ›", a link to the smallest window (30, 90, or 180 days) that contains the date, or nothing when the date is more than 180 days out.
- **A small goal page,** `/app/goals`, reached from those links and never from the nav (which stays Overview, Income, Expenses, Tags, row 44): "← Overview" and the title "Your goal". With no goal: a form with "What are you saving for?" (name), "How much do you want to have?" (amount, hint "The balance you want on that day, not an extra amount on top."), "By when?" (the calendar popover the stream form uses, days before today disabled), and "Set goal". With a goal: a card with the name as its title, the goal line and note, three small numbers ("Worst $880 · Expected $1,240 · Best $1,510 on Dec 20"), Edit (the same form in a dialog), and Delete (confirm: "Delete Trip home? kriket stops checking your forecast against it."). The card's left border is green when the expected balance meets the goal and expense-orange when it falls short, like the answer card. A page, not a card on the overview: ruling 6.
- **Dates outside the forecast:** saving a goal needs a date of today or later. Up to 366 days out (the forecast's limit) it reads as above. Further out it is kept and waits: "Dec 20, 2027 is more than a year away. kriket forecasts a year ahead, so it shows here from Dec 19, 2026.", with no target on the chart. Once the date has passed: "Dec 20 has passed. Change the date or set a new goal." until Sam edits or deletes it.
- **One goal at a time** (ruling 3). The goal page shows the form or the one goal, never a list.

**The words** live in `frontend/src/lib/goal-words.ts`, a pure function of the API's numbers in the style of `forecast-words.ts`. E, W, B are the forecast's expected, worst, and best balance on the goal date; T is the goal amount; amounts use `dollars()` (whole dollars when the cents are 00, minus sign U+2212); `{date}` is `formatDate()` ("Dec 20").

| Case | Line (tone) | Note |
|---|---|---|
| E > T | "Expected {E} by {date}, {E − T} over your {T} goal." (over) | W ≥ T: "Even the worst case gets there." · W < T: "The worst case is {T − W} short." |
| E = T | "Expected {E} by {date}, right on your {T} goal." (over) | as the row above |
| E < T | "Expected {E} by {date}, {T − E} short of your {T} goal." (short) | B ≥ T: "The best case gets there." · B < T: "Even the best case is {T − B} short." |
| Past | "{date} has passed." (none) | "Change the date or set a new goal." |
| Beyond a year | "{date with year} is more than a year away." (none) | "kriket forecasts a year ahead, so it shows here from {date − 366 days, with year}." |

When W = B (no stream in play has a range, so worst, expected, and best agree) there is no note. The story used by the tests: a check-in of $200.00 today, Shifts $100 to $200 (usually $150) every 7 days from tomorrow, a $1,000 goal 28 days from today reads "Expected $800 by {date}, $200 short of your $1,000 goal. The best case gets there."; a $500 goal on the same day reads "Expected $800 by {date}, $300 over your $500 goal. Even the worst case gets there."

Out: suggestions to meet a goal (row 35), several goals, a goal taking money out of the forecast, goals in the nav, telling Sam afterwards whether a past goal was met.

## API contract (Task 1 builds it; every other task builds against it)

Unchanged: everything that exists today, including `GET /api/forecast` and its `Forecast` type (ruling 5).

```ts
type GoalStatus = {
  state: 'forecast' | 'past' | 'beyond'; // forecast: today <= targetDate <= today + 366; past: targetDate < today; beyond: targetDate > today + 366
  expectedCents: number | null;          // the current forecast's actualCents on targetDate; null unless state = 'forecast'
  worstCents: number | null;             // its minCents on targetDate; null unless state = 'forecast'
  bestCents: number | null;              // its maxCents on targetDate; null unless state = 'forecast'
};
type Goal = {
  id: string;
  name: string;         // trimmed, 1..100 characters
  amountCents: number;  // 1..1e9: the balance Sam wants to have at the end of targetDate
  targetDate: string;   // YYYY-MM-DD
  createdAt: string;
  updatedAt: string;
  status: GoalStatus;   // computed on every read and write, never stored
};
type GoalList = { goals: Goal[]; today: string }; // zero or one goal (ruling 3); today is the server's today()
type CreateGoalBody = { name: string; amountCents: number; targetDate: string };
type UpdateGoalBody = Partial<CreateGoalBody>;
```

| Method | Path | Body | Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| GET | `/api/goals` | | `200 GoalList` |
| POST | `/api/goals` | `CreateGoalBody` | `201 Goal`; `400` on a bad field, on `targetDate` before today (path `targetDate`), or when the user already has a goal (path `goal`) |
| PATCH | `/api/goals/{id}` | `UpdateGoalBody` | `200 Goal`; `400` on a bad field or a patched `targetDate` before today; `404` when the goal is not the user's; an empty body returns the goal unchanged |
| DELETE | `/api/goals/{id}` | | `204`; `404` when the goal is not the user's |

A malformed `{id}` is a `400` from `IdParams`, as on the stream routes; signed out is `401` on all four.

**The current forecast:** the one `GET /api/forecast` returns with no `checkinId`: the latest check-in rolled forward to today (points from today; balance 0 from today when there is no check-in). A goal's status reads the point dated `targetDate` from `getForecast(userId, days)` with `days = max(7, daysBetween(today(), targetDate))`, one forecast per request covering the latest `forecast`-state goal. Because a check-in is the end-of-day balance, a goal dated today compares against today's point, which is the rolled-forward check-in.

**Table** (`backend/src/db/tables.ts`, next to `balanceCheckins`):

```ts
export const goals = pgTable(
	'goals',
	{
		id: idColumn(),
		userId: userIdColumn(),
		name: text('name').notNull(),
		amountCents: integer('amount_cents').notNull(),
		targetDate: date('target_date', { mode: 'string' }).notNull(),
		...timestamps()
	},
	// One goal per user for now (ruling 3); dropping this index is how several goals begin.
	(t) => [uniqueIndex('goals_user_idx').on(t.userId)]
);
```

**Schemas** (`backend/src/schemas/goals.ts`, each registered): `GoalStatus` and `Goal` as above; `GoalList`; `CreateGoalBody` with `name: z.string().trim().min(1).max(100)`, `amountCents: z.number().int().min(1).max(1_000_000_000)`, `targetDate: isoDate`; `UpdateGoalBody = CreateGoalBody.partial()`.

## Rulings made on the founders' behalf (undo any of these by editing this file)

1. **A goal is a balance to reach on its date,** not money set aside. Why: row 32's sketch reads it that way ("Expected $1,240 by Dec 20 … your $1,000 goal") and it needs no change to the forecast. Cost if wrong: if a goal is money put aside, the status compares a different line (the balance minus what is set aside) and the forecast changes; the table stays. Question 1.
2. **A goal does not change the forecast,** before or after its date. Why: taking the amount out on the date is a one-time expense, and one-time items are not built (row 36). Cost if wrong: after the date, the forecast still counts the money Sam spent on the thing, until the next check-in re-anchors it. Question 2.
3. **One goal at a time.** Why: with a balance to reach, two goals measure the same dollars, so a trip and a laptop could both read "over" when together they are short; one goal keeps the line honest. Cost if wrong: someone saving for two things picks one; allowing several is dropping the unique index and the service check, and deciding which goal the overview shows. Question 3.
4. **The line judges the expected balance;** the worst and best cases add one short note, and only when a range is in play. Why: the answer sentence already reads the expected line (row 5), and a note keeps the range honest without a second sentence of numbers. Cost if wrong: someone who wants certainty reads "over" while the worst case falls short; the note says so, but not first.
5. **Goal status is part of `GET /api/goals`, not of `GET /api/forecast`.** Why: the goal's date sets how far to forecast, not the overview's 30, 90, or 180 days; the balances page's forecasts from older check-ins should not carry a goal judged from them; the `Forecast` type and everything built on it stay unchanged. Cost if wrong: the overview makes one more API call and computes one more forecast per load (a few milliseconds).
6. **The goal lives on a small page, `/app/goals`, reached from the overview, not from the nav.** Why: the nav is decided (row 44); the balances page set the pattern of a page reached from an overview link; its own route lets the page and the overview be separate tasks with separate files; and it has room for the three numbers now and suggestions later (row 35). Cost if wrong: one more tap than a dialog on the overview; moving the form into a dialog reuses `goal-form.svelte` as is.
7. **A goal's date must be today or later when saved, with no upper limit;** past 366 days it waits. Why: kriket forecasts at most 366 days, and refusing a goal two years out would make Sam type it again later. Cost if wrong: a far goal sits on the overview as a line with no answer for months.
8. **A past goal says only that its date has passed,** and stays until edited or deleted. Why: whether Sam made it needs a check-in on or after the date, which Sam may not have; it is a follow-up, not the first build. Cost if wrong: Sam is not told "you made it"; a follow-up compares the first check-in on or after the date. Question 6.
9. **The target is drawn only when the goal date is inside the chart,** with a link to a window that shows it. Why: a marker pinned at the chart's edge would point at the wrong date. Cost if wrong: at 30 days a December goal is off the chart until Sam taps the link.
10. **The goal marker is neutral; met and missed borrow green and orange** on the goal page's card only. Why: green and orange mean money in and out (row 6); the balances page already uses them for "over forecast" and "under forecast". Cost if wrong: a colour change in two components.

## Open questions

1. **Is a goal a balance to reach on the date, or money set aside that the forecast stops counting as spendable?** Proposed: a balance to reach (ruling 1).
2. **When the goal date comes, does kriket take the amount out of the forecast, because Sam spends it on the thing?** Proposed: not in iteration 3 (ruling 2); first candidate once one-time items exist (row 36).
3. **One goal at a time, or several?** Proposed: one until question 2 is yes; until then two goals count the same dollars (ruling 3).
4. **Should the line call a goal met only when the worst case also meets it?** Proposed: no; the expected balance decides and the note gives the range (ruling 4).
5. **Suggestions to meet a goal (row 35, "two more shifts"): when?** Proposed: after goals ship and have been used; they go on the goal page, under the card.
6. **After the date, should kriket say whether Sam made it, from the first check-in on or after the date?** Proposed: not in iteration 3 (ruling 8).
7. **Goals more than a year out: accept and wait, or refuse?** Proposed: accept and wait (ruling 7).
8. **Does the goal page earn a place in the nav?** Proposed: no; the nav stays four items (row 44) and the overview links to it (ruling 6).
